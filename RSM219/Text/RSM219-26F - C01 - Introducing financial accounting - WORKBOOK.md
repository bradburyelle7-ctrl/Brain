#2 | External stakeholders are the main users of financial statements  
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
EXERCISE 2.1: Users of financial statements  
REFLECT: How might shareholders use financial statements? What if they didn’t have such information? 
 
  
Chapter 1 | LO 1  
Identify the users of accounting information and discuss the costs and benefits of disclosure. 
(pp. 4-7) 
 
Accounting is the process of recording, summarizing, and analysing financial transactions. It provides 
essential information for decision-makers. There are two main types of accounting:  
 Financial accounting focuses on external reporting, providing information to shareholders 
(investors), creditors, and others. It includes financial statements based on accounting standards.  
 Managerial accounting is geared towards internal decision-making. It provides company 
management with tools like budgeting, forecasting, and cost analysis. Unlike financial accounting, 
managerial accounting is not bound by external standards and is more flexible, designed to assist 
in planning, controlling, and making strategic decisions within the organization. 
 
External users of financial accounting information: 
 Common shareholders generally have the following rights associated with their share (equity) 
investments: 
1) voting rights used to elect the Board of Directors and vote on major decisions;  
2) rights to receive dividends (if they are declared); and, 
3) residual claim on the assets of the company in case of liquidation (i.e., if the company 
cannot continue to operate).  
 Creditors generally have priority rights in case of company liquidation. They often add 
"covenants" (restrictions) to further protect their rights to collect principal and interest.  
 Notice the potential conflicts of interest between shareholders (generally higher risk) and 
creditors (generally more risk-averse).

Describe a company's business activities and explain how these activities are represented by

Chapter 1 | LO 3  
Introduce the four key financial statements including the balance sheet, income statement, 
statement of changes in equity, and statement of cash flows. (pp. 11-17) 
 
 Balance sheet (or the statement of financial position) is the anchoring statement; it reports the 
financial position at a point in time. All other financial statements explain changes in some part of 
the balance sheet.  
 Income statement (or the statement of earnings/operations or statement of profit and loss) 
reports financial performance and earnings (i.e., net profit or net income). It explains some of the 
changes in equity (“earned equity”, and "retained earnings" in particular). 
 Statement of changes in equity explains changes in various components of equity (contributed 
and earned) 
 Statement of cash flows reports sources (inflows) and uses (outflows) of cash over the period. It 
is organized around 3 core business activities: operating, investing, and financing.

Chapter 12 | LO 1  
Prepare and analyse common-size financial statements. (pp. 545-550) 
 
 Common-size analysis is a technique used in financial statement analysis where each line item 
in a financial statement is expressed as a percentage of a base figure. This method allows for 
easy comparison across different periods and between companies of varying sizes by 
standardizing the financial data. 
 Vertical analysis: provides a way to understand the relative significance of each item in a 
financial statement. 
 Income statement: each line item is generally represented as a % of net sales (revenues)  
 Balance sheet: each line item is generally presented as a % of total assets 
 Horizontal analysis: involves comparing financial data across multiple periods to identify trends 
and growth patterns. This method highlights changes in financial statement items over time by 
showing the dollar and percentage increase or decrease from one period to the next.   
KEY CONCEPT: Horizontal analysis shows the 
change in each line. For example, we can calculate the 
total net sales increase in 2025 as follows:  
= (Net sales 2025 – Net sales 2024) / Net sales 2024 
= ($416,161 - $391,035) / $391,035 = 6.4% 
KEY CONCEPT: Vertical analysis shows financial statement information 
as a % of a key element such as total revenues or total assets. For 
example, we can calculate product sales as % of total revenues in 2025 
as follows:  
= Products net sales in 2025 / Total net sales in 2025 
= $307,003 / $416,161 = 73.8%
