PART 6 (2 marks) 
During September 2019, Amazon announced an order for 100,000 electric delivery vehicles from Rivian.  
REQUIRED: Would it be “material” if Rivian omitted this from their December 31, 2019, financial reports? Explain.   
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Going concern: A company is generally considered a going concern when it has sufficient liquidity, profitability, financing access, and no 
intention or need to liquidate. It may not qualify when there is substantial doubt about its survival—signaled by recurring losses, negative cash 
flows, working-capital deficiencies, debt defaults, loss of a major customer or supplier, or legal/regulatory threats to its ability to continue.  
Rivian’s going concern challenges:  
 Rivian reported ongoing losses for years. Additionally, the cost of goods sold exceeds revenues, leading to a negative gross profit until 
2024 (and only slightly positive in 2025). This appears to reflect operational inefficiencies and lack of scale. 
 Cash reserves have dropped from $18.1 billion at end of 2021 to $3.6 billion at the end of 2025, raising concerns if losses continue. 
However, “going concern” assumption is still considered reasonable given the following factors:  
 Cash Reserves: Rivian holds $3.6 billion in cash, ensuring short-term liquidity for operations and investments.  
 Growth Potential: Shifting to the expanding AI and autonomous vehicle market, Rivian has long-term prospects. 
Debt-to-equity = total liabilities / total equity = A6 / A11 = 10,270 / 4,594 = 2.24 
For each $1 of equity, Rivian had $2.24 of liabilities. The change is explained by two factors: 1) increase in liabilities as the company grows and 
expands; and 2) decrease in equity as the company accumulates losses and has an increasingly negative retained earnings posit ion (without 
raising significant funds directly from investors).  
 
Given the financial information provided for Rivian's income statements, it is not surprising that the company did not issue any dividends: 
 Net Loss: Rivian has been reporting significant net losses for the past five fiscal years, with a net loss of $3.6 billion in 2025 and a retained 
deficit balance of almost $27 billion at the end of 2025. Since dividends are typically paid out of profits, it should not be surprising that Rivian 
didn’t issue any dividends in recent years.  
 Negative Gross Profit: The company’s gross profit is positive (but only slightly) for the first time in 2025. This means that Rivian’s revenues 
are only now (barely) covering the cost of producing its vehicles. This highlights that the company is still in an early stage of scaling up 
production and has not yet achieved sustained profitability or efficiency in its operations. 
 Materiality: Information is considered material if its omission or misstatement could potentially affect stakeholders' perceptions and 
decisions regarding the company's financial health and future prospects. 
 Omission of the information about such a large order would generally be considered “material” because of the following points :  
o Amazon's order of 100,000 electric delivery vehicles represents a major revenue stream for Rivian, especially as a startup . This 
contract would significantly enhance Rivian's revenue projections and contribute to its growth trajectory.  
o Securing a large order from Amazon boosts Rivian's reputation and validates its market position.  
For more on the contract announcement, see the CNBC story from Sep. 19, 2019.
