PART 3 (4 marks) 
REQUIRED: Jordan said, “We have plenty of cash, so we are doing great!”. Do you agree? Explain.  
 
 
 
 
 
 1.2 ADJUST | Interest calculation: $50,000 loan x 12% annual interest rate = $6,000 per year or $500 per month (from Jan. 1 to Jan. 31).  
 1.3 ADJUST | Depreciation calculation: $60,000 / 5 years = $12,000 per year, or $1,000 per month.  
o Equipment is used for only half a month in January, so the amount is estimated at $1,000 x 0.5 = $500 (Jan. 15-31st).  
 1.5 ADJUST | Rent and insurance: $12,000 / 3 months = $4,000 per month 
o Rent and insurance impact for January is half a month, so the amount is estimated at $4,000 x 0.5 = $2,000 (Jan. 15 -31st).  
 
Yes, StyleHub has a significant cash balance of $105,000, but no, StyleHub Ltd. is not "doing great" just because it has plenty of cash. 
1. Imminent Liabilities Exceed Cash Balance: Despite having $105,000 in cash, StyleHub Ltd. has substantial short-term liabilities that surpass 
this amount. The company owes $150,000 in accounts payable for equipment and inventory purchased on credit, with payments due  within the 
next 30 days. Additionally, there is $15,000 in salaries payable due on February 15. This brings the total immediate obligations to $16 5,000, 
creating a cash shortfall of $60,000. The available cash is insufficient to cover these upcoming obligations, placing the company at risk of default.  
 
2. Operating at a Loss: In January, the company reported a loss of $1,000. While a loss is not unusual for a first month of operations, it is also 
not sustainable.  
 
3. High Debt Levels and Financial Risk: StyleHub Ltd. has a high debt-to-equity ratio, with total liabilities of $215,500 against total equity of 
$19,000. This results in a debt-to-equity ratio of approximately 11.3, signifying that the company is heavily leveraged. High levels of debt increase 
financial risk, making the company more vulnerable to economic downturns or operational setbacks.  
 
4. Cash Flow Misconceptions: The impressive cash balance is primarily due to initial financing activities rather than profitable operations. 
Relying on borrowed funds and investor capital to maintain cash levels is not sustainable and does not reflect operational su ccess. 
 
5. Operating Challenges and Low Profit Margins: StyleHub Ltd. faces high fixed costs, including $4,000 monthly for rent and insurance and 
$1,000 for depreciation (ongoing monthly run-rate; January reflects a half-month at $2,000 and $500 respectively). The gross profit margin is 
40%, and the net profit margin is -2%. This limits the company's ability to cover debts and invest in growth. 
 
Recommendations | To improve its financial health, StyleHub Ltd. should consider the following actions:  
 Manage Liabilities Effectively: Negotiate extended payment terms with suppliers and prioritize debt repayments to avoid defaults.  Consider 
additional financing to manage upcoming obligations.  
 Reduce Operational Expenses: Analyze all operating costs to identify areas where expenses can be cut without compromising the quality 
of products or services. 
 Improve Profit Margins: Explore opportunities to increase prices or reduce the cost of goods sold to enhance profitability.  
 And, of course, take an accounting class at Rotman!
