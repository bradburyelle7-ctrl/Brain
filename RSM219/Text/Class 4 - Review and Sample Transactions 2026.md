Understanding Transaction
On January 1st, Sporty took out a short-term loan from the bank to help finance the purchase of the point-of-sale system. The loan of 
$30,000 is repayable at the end of 6 months and carried an interest rate of 6% per year. The loan principal is payable on the maturity 
date while the interest is payable at the end of the month. The point-of-sale system has been pledged as collateral for the loan.

Understanding Transactions
On January 2nd, Sporty Store received 200 bike helmets. Each bike helmet has a retail price of $140. The helmets are 
provided on consignment. This means that the agreement between Sporty and the supplier is that Sporty will only 
pay the supplier if the helmets are sold. If the helmets are not sold, the supplier will take back the helmets at the end 
of the month.  When a helmet is sold, Sporty will keep $40 of the retail price and pay the $100 to the supplier.

Accrual Accounting: Income and Expenses are recorded as they are earned / incurred.
• Income (Revenue / Sales) → Increase in assets or a decrease in liabilities
• Revenues are incurred when a resources is earned
• Expenses → Decrease in assets or increase in liabilities
• Expenses are incurred when a resource is consumed. 
*Remember that contributions to/from holders of equity claims are not considered income or expenses.
Accrual vs. Cash Accounting
Cash Accounting:
• Cash Inflows: Recognized when company receives cash
• Cash Outflows: Recognized when company pays cash
Definition of Accrual: the accumulation 
or increase of something over time.
