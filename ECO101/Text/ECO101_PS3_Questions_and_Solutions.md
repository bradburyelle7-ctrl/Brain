The price is $30 (inﬂation adjusted so comparable) a T-shirt and she buys 10 T-shirts. Statement: Based
on these two observations, Jada’s demand curve for T-shirts is upward sloping. True, False or Uncertain?
Explain.
Solution: Uncertain. The thought experiment to derive Jada’s demand curve needs to hold all else constant
and vary only the price of T-shirts. Here there could be an income diﬀerence between the two observations
(student vs. working). Jada could have a downward sloping demand curve, but because of the
shift in income we are picking up points on two diﬀerent demand curves, which makes it look
like upward sloping demand even when it is not.
5) True/False/Uncertain. Ramen noodles are an inferior good for Houman. Statement: When Houman graduates
from college and becomes an economist, his demand for Ramen increases. True, False or Uncertain? Explain.
Solution: False. When Houman becomes an economist, his income is higher compared to when he was a student.
For an inferior good, when the income rises, the quantity demanded at the same price falls.The entire
demand curve shifts in, so his demand for Ramen decreases.

Solution: The demand for vodka shifts in, lowering price and quantity sold.
6c) The market for imitation vanilla extract: The price of vanilla beans increases. (Vanilla extract is made from
wood pulp, not the vanilla bean)
Solution: It is reasonable to assume that imitation vanilla and real vanilla extract are substitutes. Increasing the
price of vanilla beans shifts in the real vanilla extract supply curve, raising the price of real vanilla extract.
This shifts out the demand curve for imitation vanilla extract. The shift out of the demand curve means we
move up along the supply curve, sothe price and quantity transacted of imitation vanilla extract
both increase.
6d) The printer market: The price of printer ink cartridges decreases.
Solution: It is reasonable to assume printers and cartridges are complements. This shifts out the demand curve
for printers, soprice and quantity increase as we move up along the supply curve.(One way to
think about it: using a printer requires an ink cartridge. A decrease in cartridge price makes printing less
expensive, thus increasing the quantity demanded of printers at the same printer price.)
6e) The market for physical books: Amazon reduces the prices of kindle e-books.
Solution: A decrease in the price of a substitute will shift the demand curve in.As we move down the supply
curve, both price and quantity fall.
6f) Vanilla bean market: The vanilla bean crop in Madagascar (major producer of vanilla beans) failed.
Solution: The supply curve shifts in.We move up the demand curve, so price increases and quantity
decreases.
6g) Fast food : Canada falls in a recession and people lose their jobs. At the same time, new technology allows
fast food outlets to replace people with machines and reduce the cost of producing fast food. (Fast food is an
inferior good).
Solution: Losing a job means lower incomes and since fast food is inferior, this shifts the demand curve out. A
decrease in production costs, shifts supply out.Both of these increase the market quantity of fast
food, but have an ambiguous eﬀect on its price.
6h) Ontario kale: An increased awareness on the health beneﬁts of kale makes more people buy kale. Changing
climate has made Ontario land less suitable to growing kale.
Solution: More people buying kale shifts demand out. Less suitable land increases the opportunity cost of pro-
ducing kale, which decreases the number of farmers producing it (relative to the alternatives), so supply shifts
in. Both factors increase the price of kale, but the eﬀect on the market quantity of Ontario kale
is ambiguous.
6i) Elastic: COVID-19 causes an increase in the demand for cloth face masks.
Solution: Elastic is one component of a cloth face mask, which makes it a complementary input in the production
of face masks.This will increase the demand for elastic and raise both its price and the quantity
sold. Note: you could also make an argument that COVID-19 has reduced the supply of elastic because of
factory closures and disrupted global supply chains. See Toilet paper, cleaning products and now, elastic? by
Jessica Villagomez. Chicago Tribune, April 25, 2020.

7b) Students lose their part-time jobs.
Solution: Income is now lower. Demand shifts, but whether it shifts in or out depends on whether

7d) Groceries are much more expensive because fresh food prices have risen.
Solution: Demand side: the price of a related good has changed, sodemand shifts out.For the same price of a
meal, the quantity demanded increases, as cooking at home, a substitute, is now more expensive. Or for the
same quantity of meals, the Marginal Willingness to Pay (MWTP) is higher than before.

Solution: Finally,a movement along the curve!All else held equal, a change in price is a movement along the

8) Apartment rents in Toronto are very high. You may know this from personal experience. Assume that rents
are determined in a market based on the demand and supply of rental units.
You are at a recent community town hall where residents are asking the city to change zoning laws to allow
more rental apartments to be built downtown. They hope this will increase the supply of apartments and
decrease rents.
One person says that changing zoning laws this way will not solve the problem of high rents. Their argument
is that building apartments is expensive given the cost of land and construction. They say that the only rental
apartments that will be constructed will cater to very high-income renters.
Can you explain how to graph this argument? How can it be that an increase in supply will not change market
price?
Solution: Here you want to think about what determines the supply curve. A supplier will produce the extra
unit as long as their beneﬁt (rental price) is greater than their marginal cost (MC) of production. Suppose
that, due to high costs of production, every additional unit has an MC higher than the current rental price of
apartments. Even if you allowed builders to build apartments, they will only be willing to supply the additional
apartments if the price is even higher than the current market equilibrium.Supply will shift out, but
only above the current equilibrium. Since that leaves the current equilibrium unchanged, rents
will remain high.

9) The Market for Kidneys. We can’t buy and sell organs. However, there are two sides of the market–people who
need kidneys and those who would be willing to give up one kidney to a person in need (we can survive just
ﬁne with only one). Since there is no market to buy and sell kidneys, can you design a mechanism to help
people make this trade? What would it look like?
Solution: The purpose of this question is to get you to think about the role of the market and how prices determine
allocations. If we have two sides of the market, is there another mechanism, more eﬀective and requiring
minimal outside intervention, to allocate goods to the people with the highest value (highest MWTP)? At the
same time you need to identify people willing to give up the good and match them together to make a trade
that makes both sides better oﬀ.
