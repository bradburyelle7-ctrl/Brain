19) [3 points] Assume that new houses are a normal good transacted in a perfectly competitive market. For 2026
to 2027, there was no change in the market price of a new home, but the quantity transacted increased. The
change from 2026 to 2027 can be explained by in incomes and in the price of lumber used in
housing construction.
A. an increase; a decrease
B. an increase; an increase
C. a decrease; a decrease
D. a decrease; an increase
E. an increase; no change
Solution: A. The change in income shifts the demand curve, while the change in the price of lumber shifts
the supply curve. To increase the quantity transacted, demand must shift out (incomes must increase, as
houses are a normal good) and supply must also increase (a decrease in MC due to a decrease in lumber
prices). Each shift on its own would move the price, but they move it in opposite directions: if the two
shifts cancel out exactly, we get no change in price alongside an increase in market quantity.

Q2) Barbie, Skipper and Alan live together and split their time between two chores — tidying up rooms and
cooking meals. Each of them has 5 hours a week to spend on chores.
Q2a) [5 points] Claim: If all three can cook 2 meals in one hour, they must have the same Opportunity Cost of
an extra meal. Agree, disagree, or it depends? Explain your reasoning.
Solution: Disagree. Cooking at the same rate does not mean facing the same Opportunity Cost.
Each of them requires half an hour for an extra meal. However, the Opportunity Cost of that meal is
the number of tidy rooms given up, and that depends on how many rooms each of them can tidy in half
an hour. They will have the same Opportunity Cost only if they also tidy the same number of rooms in
an hour.
Q2b) [6 points] Right now, as a group, they are efficiently producing 14 meals a week with the rest of their time
devoted to tidying rooms. Alan is doing only cooking. Barbie is doing only tidying.Claim: If they need an
extra meal, Barbie should produce it. Agree, disagree, or it depends? Explain your reasoning.
Solution: It depends — and usually the claim is wrong.
If the group is producing efficiently and Barbie is only tidying, it must be that she has thehighest
Opportunity Cost of meal production. She should not be the one switching if Skipper still has time split
between tidying and cooking: Skipper has a lower Opportunity Cost and should be switched in first.
The only reason to switch Barbie in is if Skipper is already cooking full time and has no more time to
move into cooking. Since the question doesn’t tell us how Skipper’s time is split, we can’t rule that out.
