Individuals to Market Curves
• Individuals look at a market price and decide if 
they want to buy (or sell) at that price
• Market demand (and supply) curves keep track 
of the numbers of buyers (and sellers) at 
different market prices, all else held fixed

Recall: Extension Question from Week 1
• Five hundred of you worked out the lowest wage that 
would get you out to shovel snow. Then I asked 
what you would have drawn if I lined all 500 up, 
lowest first, and counted how many would work at 
each wage.
• You would have drawn a supply curve.
• A supply curve is a queue of people, sorted by the 
minimum they would need to be paid to provide a 
good or service. 
• As the price you offer them increases, more people 
find that the price moves the Benefit vs. Opp. Cost 
comparison into the accept decision category.

everyone’s classes and shifts?

people in the accept decision category changes

Summary
ECO101: Principles of Microeconomics, Prof. Freitas 12
• Market demand (supply) adds up quantity 
demanded (supplied) for all consumers 
(producers) at the market price 
• A change in market price, all else same, moves 
us along the curve. 
• If for the same price, quantity bought or sold 
changes, the entire curve shifts. Shifts happen 
either because each person's cutoff changed, or 
because the set of people changed
