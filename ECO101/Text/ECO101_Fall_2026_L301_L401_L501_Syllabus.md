1 Course Overview
You have an unscheduled hour on a Saturday morning. Rare “free” time, right? Not quite. You could
binge-watch something, sleep, visit a friend, or study. Picking one means giving up the chance to do the
next best thing. Your “free” time has anopportunity cost, a central concept in economics.
ECO101 introduces you to a few general principles economists use to think through choices people and
ﬁrms make. We examine the patterns those choices produce in markets, and how markets respond when
conditions change. For example: what happens to laptop prices when data centres expand rapidly? Who
eﬀectively pays a new $1 gas tax—drivers or gas stations? Does a higher minimum wage lead to job losses?
We answer questions like these by starting from assumptions that make a complicated reality manageable,
so we can focus on the mechanisms that matter. Knowing which assumptions your predictions rest on is
what lets you adapt your reasoning to new situations and recognize the limitations of your analysis.
That skill only comes from doing it. You’ll learn how to problem-solve using economic principles by
working through questions on your own, and with your classmates in workshops and tutorials. In short,
lots of practice! When opportunity cost thinking becomes second nature, you’re on your way to becoming
an economist.
1.1 Learning Objectives
By the end of the course, you should be able to:
• Set up and solve choice problems for individuals and ﬁrms, adapting them to speciﬁc questions and
contexts.
• Explain how individuals and ﬁrms interacting in a market determine what gets produced, who gets
it, and at what price, and evaluate whether the outcome is eﬃcient.
• Given a scenario you haven’t seen before, identify and use the appropriate ECO101 concepts to reach
an answer.
• Present your reasoning using math, graphs, and clear English, and move between them.
• Critically analyze economic arguments using course concepts, explaining where and why the logic
holds or breaks down.

2 Graded Work
2.1 Academic Integrity
Every piece of graded work in this course measures how far along you are at reaching the learning objectives
in section 1.1. It only means something if it is yours and completed unaided. Work completed with help
from a tutor, a website, a classmate, or a generative AI tool does not capture your achievement.
• Unless told otherwise,any work submitted for grades must be completed on your own
without assistance. No discussing any part of it, no looking up answers, no soliciting or receiving
help from tutors or classmates. Workshop participation is an exception because collaboration with
classmates during workshops is expected.No generative AI tools or assistance for graded
work.
• Participation work is graded work. It’s designed to give you chances to test your knowledge
and ﬁx gaps before they show up on tests. Don’t waste those chances by taking shortcuts.
Why this matters for you
• Economics is sequenced.The courses after this one are built on what you’re learning in this one,
and they don’t re-teach it. Shortcuts now compound gaps quietly, and closing them later takes far
more work than learning things properly the ﬁrst time.
• In a world with AI, your ability to do the higher-order thinking is especially valuable.
You cannot build it without a strong foundation. If you have ever struggled with harder math,
denser readings, or more complicated writing because you never quite nailed the basics, you already
know what I mean.

2.2 How to do well in ECO101
Before we get to details on graded work, let me address the biggest source of anxiety for students in this
course. You already know the answer to succeeding in this course. Think of anything outside of academics
you’re good at. How did you get good at it? The answer is usually regular deliberate practice, learning
from mistakes and time management.
In my experience, when eﬀort doesn’t show up in your grades it is typically because your study strategies
need adjustment. What worked in high school often does not transfer to university. Quercus has more
resources and advice to help you with this. Learning strategists in the Academic Success Centre or in
Rotman Commerce, can help. It is worth a single appointment early rather than a post-midterm crisis.

than the drops cover, talk to your registrar. Take documentation forall missed work, including the
