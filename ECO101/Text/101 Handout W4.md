Why not “the price went up by $1.50”?
• Absolute changes depend on the units and where 
you started
• $1.50 on a gallon of gas is enormous. $1.50 on a used EV 
is nothing.
• Elasticity  = % change in the quantity that responded
% change in the one thing that moved
• Unit-free: comparable across markets, time, etc. 
• Notation: Δ for change

Discrete vs. continuous data
• Discrete data: Last week: five buyers, five 
sellers, demand/supply as a staircase.
• Continuous data: Five hundred buyers and the 
steps get smaller. The staircase becomes a line.
• We can use slopes. Careful: slope is not the elasticity 
• Choose the appropriate formula for the data 
provided: 
• midpoint method: discrete data
• point method: continuous data

Summary
ECO101: Principles of Microeconomics, Prof. Freitas 11
• Elasticity measures how quantity responds to a 
change in one factor, all else held fixed
• Along a linear demand curve, the unit elastic 
point is spending or revenue maximizing
• Spending and revenue are the same number (P × Q) 
seen from the buyer's side and the seller's side of the 
market
• In a competitive market nobody chooses where on 
the curve we sit; the market does 
• Revenue-maximizing price is not the profit-
maximizing one as revenue ignores costs

What makes the response big or small?
• W3: Markets are aggregations of individuals 
making choices
• If we want to understand why quantities 
respond, we think through how and why choices 
change
For example, when prices increase: are there 
other things I can buy? Do I want to produce 
more given my costs? How much time to adjust?

Summary
ECO101: Principles of Microeconomics, Prof. Freitas 22
• Identify the reasons behind market changes to 
determine which elasticity to use to predict 
changes in prices, quantities, or 
revenue/spending
• When you want to use a given elasticity:
• Make sure the denominator is the only factor changing, all 
else held fixed
• Ask, why does the quantity in the numerator change? What 
determines the size of the change?
• If you want to interpret it on a demand/supply 
diagram, ask, does it cause a movement along 
the curve or of the curve? Why?
