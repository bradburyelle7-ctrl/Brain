1) [2 points] Your values expressed in dollars from three entertainment options tonight are $80 for a concert, $70
to watch a tennis match, and $60 for dinner. All of them are priced at $40. You can go to only one. Based
on this information, what is the opportunity cost of going to dinner?
A. $120
B. $80
C. $70
D. $40
E. None of the above.
Solution: B. If you go to the dinner, your next best option is going to the concert. Your Opportunity Cost
is the $40 you spend plus the net benefit you give up from the concert:$40 + ($80− $40) = $80.

Q1) Assume perfectly competitive markets with typical curves. At Halloween, consumers choose between two types
of candy to give trick-or-treaters: chocolate bars and gummy candies. Chocolate bars are made with cocoa.
Gummy candies do not contain any cocoa. A severe drought in cocoa producing areas has increased the price
of cocoa.
Q1a) [5 points] Claim: As gummy candy production doesn’t use cocoa, the market price of gummy candy will
not change when cocoa prices rise. Agree, disagree, or it depends? Explain. A diagram is not an explanation.
Solution: Disagree. The gummy market is linked to the chocolate market through demand, not through

An increase in the MC of chocolate shifts chocolate supply up and increases chocolate prices. Consumers
consider gummy candy and chocolate candy substitutes, since they choose between them. As the price
of chocolate bars rises, people’s MWTP — and hence demand — for gummy candies increases. As the
demand curve moves out, we move up along the supply curve for gummy candy to a higher price for
gummy candy this Halloween.

Q2) People choose between streaming music online or buying vinyl records and listening to music on their record
player. Harmonix is an online music streaming service. For $11 a month, subscribers to Harmonix can listen
to as many songs as they want, with no additional payment. Consumers are different, but every consumer’s
marginal willingness to pay for songs streamed is decreasing in the number of songs every month. Harmonix
pays artists $0.01 every time their song is streamed.
Q2a) [5 points] Vinyl records have suddenly become more popular thanks to social media. Assume subscribers
continue to buy the $11 a month Harmonix subscription. Does the increased popularity of vinyl affect the
number of songs they stream a month? Why or why not? Explain your reasoning.
Solution: Yes — they stream fewer songs, even though nothing about the price has changed.
The price of an extra streamed song is zero, and it remains zero. But if the next best option to a
streamed song — listening to it on vinyl — becomes better, the MWTP for streamed songs decreases
at every quantity. At the same price of zero for an extra song, when MWTP decreases fewer units cross
the MWTP-versus-price threshold. Consumers will choose to stream fewer songs a month than before.
Q2b) [6 points] In December, Harmonix plans to raise the subscription price to $14 a month.Claim: Artists
will earn less money from Harmonix because of fewer streams. Agree, disagree, or it depends? Explain your
reasoning.
Solution: Agree — but the streams are lost because people leave, not because the people who stay listen
less.
Each individual who chooses to continue subscribing faces the same price of an extra song, zero, and
their MWTP is unchanged, so they choose exactly the same number of songs. Same quantity means
their total benefit is the same, while their total cost is now higher, since the fixed subscription price is
higher.
So there will now be people for whom, at the same quantity as before, total benefit is no longer enough
to cover the higher $14 subscription fee. They will not subscribe to Harmonix any more. It is those lost
subscribers, not reduced listening by the ones who stay, that reduce the number of streams and hence
artists’ earnings.

Q3b) [5 points] Xin, who was willing to pay $2,000 for the ticket, did not get a ticket from the lottery. Yana, who
was willing to pay $750 for the ticket, did get a ticket from the lottery.Claim: Allowing Yana to sell her

If Yana doesn’t sell the ticket to Xin, her next best option is attending the concert and getting $750 in

cannot determine how the individual surpluses change, because we don’t know what price the trade
happens at — but that price is a transfer and doesn’t affect the total.
Q3c) [5 points] Assume that any ticket resale must take place at the posted price of $500 and this policy can be

Solution: It kills the trade entirely, so total surplus is unchanged.
From part (b) we know that for the trade to happen the price needs to be between $750 and $2,000.

concert and getting $750 of value. As the trade will not happen, total surplus remains unchanged — the
$1,250 gain identified in part (b) is simply not realized.
