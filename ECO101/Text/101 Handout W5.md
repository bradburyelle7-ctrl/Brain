Efficiency
Market Allocation: Quantity traded and who bought/ 
produced the goods traded
A market or any allocation is efficient when 
1. For the goods already being traded, no reshuffling 
of who holds/produced could make someone better 
off without making someone else worse off
2. We are trading all good for which the benefit to 
society is at least as high as the social cost of 
producing it, and none for which it is lower.  
Notice: W2 concepts of gains from trade. Does not 
consider fairness or prices

Misallocation: Why the Demand and 
Supply diagram can’t see it
• W3: market demand is a queue of buyers, 
sorted from highest MWTP to lowest.
• The area under demand up to Q assumes the Q 
units went to the first Q people in that queue.
• Lottery: Q = 3. On the Demand (and Supply) 
diagram since everyone is already in order, we 
can’t see if the allocation differs.
• The loss only shows when you look at who holds 
the units: a gap in the queue. F2 has no seat, 
F5 has one.

--- Page 22 ---
Markets are Efficient
“The market outcome is efficient” true if:
• Everyone is a price taker, and prices are free to 
move
• Buyers and sellers have full information
• Nobody outside the trade is affected by it
• Trade is voluntary, and no one is excluded by 
anything but price
We need to check if markets are efficient, not assume 
they always are

Summary
ECO101: Principles of Microeconomics, Prof. Freitas 23
|DWL|=Efficient Total Surplus−Allocation surplus
Deadweight Loss (DWL) arises from two sources
1. Over/under production
• Units with a net gain ≥ 0 to society not produced
• Units with a net gain < 0 to society produced
• Notice only quantity important, not price
2. Misallocation: Keeping quantity fixed, 
reallocating the good either across buyers or 
sellers (or both) increases total surplus
• Typically, there is an incentive to trade in such cases 
if allowed/possible
