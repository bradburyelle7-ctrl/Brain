Why Opportunity Costs?
• Scarce resources + many opportunities ⇒ how to
allocate resources?
• Choosing to allocate resource to one option means
giving up another option.
• ⇒ we need to include forgone opportunity into the cost
of taking an action.

Opportunity Costs
• The Opportunity Cost of an action is the net value
(expressed in $s) of the next best alternative.
• Ignore deﬁnition, focus on what it means. Adapt the
general principle to your setting.

Explicit vs. Implicit Costs
Economic costs = Opportunity Costs (OC)
• Explicit costs: costs that you actually pay (think price
paid)
• Implicit costs: costs that are relevant to economic
decision making but not something you can get a receipt
for. e.g. time spent reading book.

Economic vs Accounting Costs
• Opportunity Costs = Explicit + Implicit Costs
• Economic proﬁts = Revenue − Opportunity Costs
• Accounting Costs = Explicit costs.
• Accounting proﬁts = Revenue − Accounting Costs

Sunk Costs
• Sunk costs are costs that have already been spent (or
committed to) and you cannot recover (or get out of
paying).
• They should not aﬀect choices.
• If you cannot reallocate the resource, it can’t be used
anywhere else and so has no impact on opportunity cost.

Zero (Economic) Proﬁts
Zero Economic Proﬁts
• Economic Proﬁts = Revenues - Opportunity Costs
• Zero Economic Proﬁts ⇒ Revenues = Opportunity Costs
• You are making as much as your next best alternative!

Marginal Costs and Beneﬁts
MC: Marginal Cost, MB: Marginal Beneﬁt
You have N − 1 units and are considering buying 1 extra unit
which will get you to N units
• MC: Increase in costs when purchase the extra unit.
MC (N)= Cost(N)− Cost(N − 1)
• MB: Increase in beneﬁts when purchase the extra unit.
MB(N)= Beneﬁt(N)− Beneﬁt(N − 1)
