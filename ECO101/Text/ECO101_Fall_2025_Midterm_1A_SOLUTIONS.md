Q3) Assume all markets are perfectly competitive with the usual market demand and supply curves. Farmers raise
chickens that lay eggs (sold in the egg market). They also sell some chickens as meat (chicken market). Bird
flu kills a large fraction of chickens.
Q3a) [5 points] Claim: Prices in the egg market will fall due to the bird flu. Agree, disagree, or it depends?
Explain your reasoning.
Solution: Disagree. The supply of eggs comes from chickens. If the chickens die then there are fewer eggs in
the market at every market price; equivalently, the MC of an egg is higher at every quantity. This shifts
the market supply curve up/in. The demand curve is unchanged, so we move up the demand curve to
a higher price and a lower market quantity. At the old market price there would be excess demand for
eggs, and price would have to rise to bring quantity demanded back in line with quantity supplied.
Q3b) [6 points] After the bird flu, spending on eggs rises while spending on chicken meat falls.Claim: This is
because consumers have more alternatives for chicken meat than for eggs. Agree, disagree, or it depends?
Explain.
Solution: Agree. When consumers have more alternatives their demand is more elastic, because they can
switch away more easily when the price rises. This does not necessarily mean demand for chicken is
elastic in levels; it could simply be more elastic than demand for eggs. Whether we can use the spending
data depends on what is moving.
In both markets the bird flu shifts supply up/in: fewer surviving chickens means fewer chickens (and
fewer eggs) brought to market at the same price, and the cost of an extra chicken or egg is higher at
every quantity. Demand is unchanged in both markets, so we move up the demand curve to a higher
price and a lower quantity. Because this is a movement along demand, we can use demand elasticity to
sign the change in spending.
Spending is P× Q. Spending on eggs rises when price rises, so demand for eggs is inelastic. Spending on
chicken falls when price rises, so demand for chicken is elastic. Chicken demand being more elastic than
egg demand is exactly what having more alternatives would produce, so the claim is consistent with the
data.

Q4) FIFA must set ticket prices for World Cup 2026 matches. Assume: (1) all tickets are priced at $100 each, (2)
each match has 1,000 seats, and (3) at a price of $100, many more than 1,000 tickets are demanded. Current
plan: tickets are sold online, and the first 1,000 people to log in may buy one ticket each.
Q4a) [5 points] Proposal A: Everyone who wants a $100 ticket signs up online. 1,000 names are randomly drawn
and may purchase one ticket each.Claim: Producer Surplus will increase under Proposal A relative to the
current plan. Agree, disagree, or it depends? Explain.
Solution: Disagree. Producer Surplus is the difference between price and MC for every unit sold. Under
Proposal A the same 1,000 seats are sold, so quantity is unchanged, and the cost of producing those seats
does not depend on who sits in them. They are all still sold at the same $100 price. Producer Surplus
is therefore unchanged.
Q4b) [6 points] Ignore part (a).Proposal B: Set the ticket price so that exactly 1,000 people are willing to buy
a ticket at that price.Claim: Consumer Surplus will increase under Proposal B relative to the current plan.
Agree, disagree, or it depends? Explain.
Solution: It depends. Consumer Surplus is the difference between MWTP andP for every unit sold. We
have the same 1,000 seats, so quantity stays the same, but the price is no longer $100: since quantity
demanded at $100 exceeds 1,000, the price must rise untilQD(P ) = 1,000. This higher price reduces CS.
The people sitting in the seats also change. At $100 there was potential misallocation: some people with
a higher MWTP did not get a ticket, which reduced CS conditional on price. With the higher price only
1,000 people demand tickets, and these are the 1,000 highest-MWTP buyers. Misallocation is eliminated,
which either raises the sum of MWTP or leaves it the same.
Since the sum of MWTP rises (or stays the same) andP rises, the net impact on Consumer Surplus is
uncertain.
