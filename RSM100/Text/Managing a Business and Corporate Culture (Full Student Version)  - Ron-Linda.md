WHAT DETERMINES THE 
RIGHT STRUCTURE?
There is no single “best” structure. Every design 
creates trade-offs.
Structure follows strategy. It should support 
what the organization is trying to accomplish. 
Context matters. Size, industry, geography, 
regulation and pace of change all influence the 
design. 
Structures evolve. Organizations reorganize as 
their strategy, markets and complexity change. 
Most organizations are hybrids. One pattern 
may dominate, but elements are often 
combined.

--- Page 30 ---
THERE ARE DIFFERENT TYPES OF ORGANIZATIONAL STRUCTURES
The Primary Structures organize the work. Design choices shape how each pattern operates.
FUNCTIONAL
GROUPED BY EXPERTISE
People with similar expertise sit 
together.
Best for specialization and 
consistency.
EXAMPLE: APPLE
DIVISIONAL
GROUPED BY MARKET

customer group.
Best for large, diversified 
organizations.

complex work.
EXAMPLE: KRAFT HEINZ
NETWORK

A core organization coordinates 
internal and external partners.
Best for flexibility and access to 
partners.
EXAMPLE: NIKE
DESIGN CHOICES INFLUENCE HOW THAT STRUCTURE OPERATES

CULTURE IS DEEPER THAN WHAT YOU CAN SEE
EDGAR SCHEIN’S THREE LEVELS OF ORGANIZATIONAL CULTURE
SEE ARTIFACTS
SAY ESPOUSED VALUES
BELIEVE UNDERLYING ASSUMPTIONS
DEEPER  =  LESS VISIBLE  +  MORE INFLUENTIAL
ARTIFACTS
What you see and hear
Spaces, symbols, rituals and behaviours. Easy to spot; 
hard to interpret without context.
ESPOUSED VALUES
What the organization says matters
Its stated mission, goals, values, philosophies and 
policies.
UNDERLYING ASSUMPTIONS
What people really believe
Taken-for-granted beliefs and unspoken rules that 
actually drive everyday decisions and behaviour.
THE VISIBLE CLUES MATTER—but the deepest layer usually explains how things really work.

7 MOST COMMON LEADERSHIP STYLES
Style One-liner Best used in Potential Watch outs
Autocratic “I decide” Speed or crisis situations Low engagement
Democratic “We decide” You need buy-in or input Slower decisions
Transformational “Let’s change the future” Driving vision or change Can miss details
Transactional “Do this → get that” Clear goals, performance 
focus Limits innovation
Servant “I support you” Building trust, developing 
teams Blurred boundaries
Laissez-faire “You own it” Skilled, self-directed 
teams Lack of direction
Coaching “Let me grow you” Developing individuals Time intensive
