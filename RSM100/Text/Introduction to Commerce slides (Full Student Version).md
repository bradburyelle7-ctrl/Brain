Your Phone Doesn’t Have to Buzz to Hurt You

All phones were off.
01

Worst performance
02

--- Page 28 ---
F R A M E W O R K  0 1
The 3 R’s — Redesign the System
Willpower fails. Environment design doesn’t. Stop fighting your phone — remove it from the fight.
Reach
Keep distraction physically out of reach. Phone in another room, not face-down on the desk.
Roadblock
Make distraction expensive. Delete the app instead of muting it. Every layer of friction is a win.
Rituals
Pre-decide your next action. Vague goals fail 62% of the time. Rituals fail only 9% of the time.
Source: Peter Gollwitzer, NYU — implementation intentions research

F R A M E W O R K  0 2
SOS — Train Your Focus Like a Muscle
You cannot bench your max weight on day one. Start small, recover fully, lock it in.
Sprint
Start with 15–20 minutes of focus, 
not 90. Even 7 minutes is a real 
starting point.
Offload
Go fully dark after a sprint. No phone. 
A short walk measurably recharges 
cognitive capacity.
Save
10 seconds of stillness mid -sprint 
helps you encode what you learned 
up to 20x faster .
Source: Gloria Mark (recovery walks) — encoding-speed effect of brief stillness during work sprints

What is this course 
about?
➢ “Introduction to Management”
• Managing people (human resource management), managing 
money (finance), managing the processes of marketing  goods 
and services, managing operations, analyzing the environment, 
planning for the future
• These are all areas of considerable specialization - yet all 
managers need to understand the basics
• Some of these areas will become key to your success as you 
progress in your careers
--- Page 81 ---
What else is this 
course about?
• This course marks the beginning of an educational 
process in management – introduction to some of 
the theories, professional skills, and practices of 
“organizations”  
• University studies in management – an academic 
environment as well as “professional business 
training”
➢ Making Choices – the decision to simply “chase 
marks” while at University, OR to learn, change 
and develop  – you must make the decision
--- Page 82 ---
Why study business/ management?
• Many of you will ultimately work for businesses 
in management functions; some will work for 
businesses which you own
– Others will analyze businesses – financial/ 
investment analysis, business journalism
• Yet others will manage – people, information, 
processes… but outside for-profit business – 
government, not-for-profit organizations
• Difficult to know what careers will be like in the 
future, given the pace of change in the 
economy & society
– Key question to ask yourselves: What 
knowledge & skills can be acquired over 
next 4 years to create personal & 
professional capabilities that will last?
