Martin Eichler
BComm Finance Specialist, MBA Strategy & Leadership Emphasis
Sr. Director of Commercialization Strategy
Moved to Canada from Europe for my BComm at Rotman Commerce, and remained in my comfort zone sticking mostly to myself in the first year.
Recognized that I need to stand out among my peers to get unique career-amplifying opportunities as everyone at Rotman has high intelligence and has impressive CVs. I realized that those alone will not get me to where I want to go.
Started to get out of my shell in my second year, went to Summer Abroad and started making connections with fellow students and Prof. Michael Khan.
Leveraged the connection with Prof. Khan to become his Research Assistant Team Lead and later his Lead TA for RSM/MGT100.
Close to the end of my 4th year, I met the Sr. VP of Supply Chain of Coca-Cola Canada, holding a guest lecture in RSM100. As Lead TA, I helped the guest speaker coordinate the logistics for the day and built a new connection. After the guest lectures, I let him know I am looking for an internship or entry level role and that I am interested in Coca-Cola. He offered me to recommend my CV to his HR team to see whether there might be a role for me.
That helped me secure a Sales internship at Coca-Cola, which turned into a full time Sales Analyst job.
9 years later, I am the Sr. Director of Commercialization Strategy, leading a team of 7 Directors responsible for our North America Water, Tea & Coffee portfolio at our Global Head Office in Atlanta. Each promotion throughout my career was enabled by the network I built, in most cases ahead of applying to new roles. The people I wanted to work for already knew me and my professional contributions.
"My advice is to build a network before you need a job or help. Already having the relationship when you do, makes asking for support less transactional.
Get ahead of it!"

--- Slide 80 ---
What is this course about?
“Introduction to Management”
Managing people (human resource management), managing money (finance), managing the processes of marketing goods and services, managing operations, analyzing the environment, planning for the future
These are all areas of considerable specialization - yet all managers need to understand the basics
Some of these areas will become key to your success as you progress in your careers
--- Slide 81 ---
What else is this course about?
This course marks the beginning of an educational process in management – introduction to some of the theories, professional skills, and practices of “organizations”
University studies in management – an academic environment as well as “professional business training”
Making Choices – the decision to simply “chase marks” while at University, OR to learn, change and develop  – you must make the decision
--- Slide 82 ---
Why study business/ management?
Many of you will ultimately work for businesses in management functions; some will work for businesses which you own
Others will analyze businesses – financial/ investment analysis, business journalism
Yet others will manage – people, information, processes… but outside for-profit business – government, not-for-profit organizations
Difficult to know what careers will be like in the future, given the pace of change in the economy & society
Key question to ask yourselves: What knowledge & skills can be acquired over next 4 years to create personal & professional capabilities that will last?
