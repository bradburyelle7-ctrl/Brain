--- Slide 28 ---
Organizational structure is the living blueprint of an organization
It defines how work is divided, who has authority, how teams coordinate and where decisions get made
--- Slide 29 ---
What determines the right structure?
There is no single “best” structure. Every design creates trade-offs.
Structure follows strategy. It should support what the organization is trying to accomplish.
Context matters. Size, industry, geography, regulation and pace of change all influence the design.
Structures evolve. Organizations reorganize as their strategy, markets and complexity change.
Most organizations are hybrids. One pattern may dominate, but elements are often combined.
--- Slide 30 ---
THERE ARE DIFFERENT TYPES OF ORGANIZATIONAL STRUCTURES
The Primary Structures organize the work. Design choices shape how each pattern operates.
FUNCTIONAL
GROUPED BY EXPERTISE
People with similar expertise sit together.
Best for specialization and consistency.
EXAMPLE: APPLE
DIVISIONAL
GROUPED BY MARKET
Units focus on a product, region or customer group.
Best for large, diversified organizations.
EXAMPLE: DISNEY
MATRIX
GROUPED TWO WAYS
Functions work across brands, projects or regions.
Best for sharing expertise across complex work.
EXAMPLE: KRAFT HEINZ
NETWORK
CONNECTED THROUGH PARTNERS
A core organization coordinates internal and external partners.
Best for flexibility and access to partners.
EXAMPLE: NIKE
DESIGN CHOICES INFLUENCE HOW THAT STRUCTURE OPERATES
FLAT
Fewer management layers
DECENTRALIZED
Decisions sit closer to the work
AGILE
Teams adapt through short work cycles

What is corporate culture?
--- Slide 37 ---
Corporate culture is the invisible current running through the organization
The shared assumptions, values and norms that shape how people behave, make decisions and work together. It is shaped by
INDUSTRY &
ENVIRONMENT
The pace, risks, rules and expectations surrounding the organization.
LEADERSHIP
What leaders model, prioritize, reward—and tolerate.
PEOPLE &
PRACTICES
Who gets hired and promoted, plus the stories, symbols, rituals and everyday behaviours that are reinforced.
WHY IT MATTERS
Culture guides behaviour—especially in spaces where no formal policy & process are telling people what to do.

--- Slide 53 ---
7 most common leadership styles
Style | One‑liner | Best used in | Potential Watch outs
Autocratic | “I decide” | Speed or crisis situations | Low engagement
Democratic | “We decide” | You need buy‑in or input | Slower decisions
Transformational | “Let’s change the future” | Driving vision or change | Can miss details
Transactional | “Do this → get that” | Clear goals, performance focus | Limits innovation
Servant | “I support you” | Building trust, developing teams | Blurred boundaries
Laissez‑faire | “You own it” | Skilled, self-directed teams | Lack of direction
Coaching | “Let me grow you” | Developing individuals | Time intensive
