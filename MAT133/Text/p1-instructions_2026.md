1 Introduction
Project 1 is based on the learning goals from Weeks 1 - 4. As a pod, you will be analyzing rates of change of a
real data set of your choice, in order to address a research question of your choice.

Instructions.

and general formatting, for Project 1 specifically, we will pay special attention to the formatting of your
Final Report.
This document outlines specific instructions for this project, as well as a partial rubric for the focus on formatting.
The Appendix contains reliable sources of data that you may wish to use for your analysis.

3 Pod Project 1: Analyzing Change in Real Data
In Project 1, your pod will explore a question of genuine interest using real-world data. In particular, you will use
rates of change to analyze a dataset of your choice and gain new insights from the information it contains.
3.1 Finding a Data Set
Remember, this part of the project is to be completed in your pods. You will need to find a data set that you are
all interested in analyzing. Please consider the following notes as you search for your data set.
• The data set should consist of at least two columns of data, with one column containing values of an input
variable and at least one column containing values of an output variable.
• We recommend choosing a data set with time as the input variable. However, if you wish to study a data
set with a different input variable, please ask your tutorial TA or ask the teaching team in a private Piazza
post for advice and approval before starting. In your post, please explain why you’re interested in the rates
of change for your specific data set.
• Depending on your research question, you may wish to look at multiple data sets and combine them.
• Data sets are commonly found online in the form of a CSV file, which can be opened in Microsoft Excel
(available for free to UofT students).
• A few sources are listed in the appendix, but you are not restricted to these sources. You will be required to
summarize the survey or experiment that resulted in this data set.
• All pods within your hive should use their own distinct data sets! In particular, it is your job to ensure that
every pod in your hive is using a different data set before you begin your analysis. You may communicate
this with each other in your tutorials, Quercus group page, or through another channel of your choice.
Important: In order to genuinely answer your research question, you will need to make sure you use real
data from a reliable source. While some websites contain a lot of real datasets, they may also contain fictitious
datasets. You should not assume that data posted on one of the sites in the Appendix is automatically
suitable! Instead, look for information on how the data was collected to verify that the data is real and
reliable.
You might have questions about a data set, such as:
• Is this source reliable?
• How can I open this data in Excel?
• How can I convert this file to a format that Excel recognizes?
• What counts as a “distinct” data set, when comparing with other pods in my hive?
In this case, ask your TA during tutorials or post to Piazza in the “Projects” category. If sharing detailed information
about your ideas or your report, we recommend making your post private to avoid an academic offence.

The same instructions for the Final Report apply to the First Draft. Your pod will be given credit for submitting a

3.3 Final Report Instructions
As you collaborate on this project, please write a report consisting of four sections that address the requirements
outlined below. Aim for the final draft of your report to be both engaging and easily comprehensible to any other
student in the course. Any graphs, data tables, or formulas you introduce should always be preceded by text that
provides context for these items. Ensure that the report reads fluidly and appears cohesive throughout all sections.
Note that you are not constrained to a specific number of words or pages. However, keep in mind that any extra
information and/or context you include will be graded to the same standard as the rest of the report.

Caution: Do not use ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot, or any other generative AI to generate your report.
These tools can give factually inaccurate information and severely lack depth in their analyses. Writing the
report with your pod (and without AI) is essential to your learning experience in this project.
1. Introduction
The purpose of this section is to introduce your project, explain the motivation for your investigation, and
provide the background information needed for a reader to understand the analysis that follows.
(a) Please begin by stating your research question: this is a question that you aim to answer by analyzing
your data set and its rates of change. Explain why your pod is interested in answering this question.
Your language here should be non-technical and understandable by the general public.
(b) Find at least one article or source published by a reliable outlet (such as a government agency,
academic journal, or reputable news organization) that is related to your research question. Write 2–3
sentences summarizing what this source says and how it provides context for your research question. Be
sure to include a proper in-line citation and add the source to your bibliography.
(c) What is the meaning and relevance of your chosen data set? Write a paragraph (or a few) to
explain the meaning and relevance of your data set. Your paragraphs should address the following (in
your preferred order):
• Include a properly formatted table of values displaying the first 10 rows of your data set.
• What do the numbers in your data set represent? Include units.
• Which variable do you consider as the independent (or input) variable? Which is the dependent (or
output) variable?
• Where did you obtain this data set? Provide a proper in-line citation and add it to you bibliography.
• Briefly summarize the experiment or survey that resulted in this data set.
• Why is this data set well suited to the question your pod hopes to explore?
2. Visual Representation of Your Dataset
In this section, you will create at least one visual representation of your data set (beyond the table of values
of the first 10 rows of data), using Excel or another computational tool of your choice, and describe the
behaviour of the graph as it relates to your research question.
• Create a scatterplot, with the input variable on the horizontal axis and the output variable on the vertical
axis. In this case, start by constructing a scatterplot that is interesting to your pod. You may use Excel,
Octave Online, or any other platform of your choice.
– Please be sure that the axes on your plot are labelled properly, including units.
– Please do not include a line of best fit (or any other curve of best fit) on your scatterplot.
– In your report, include a short paragraph before your graph, to introduce your graph to the reader.
– Make sure your scatterplot and other figures are properly captioned.
• Please write a short paragraph to describe the behaviour of your function(s) based on the graph(s),
as it relates to your research question. If you feel it appropriate, you may include additional visual
representation(s) to accompany your paragraph (in addition to your scatterplot). Your language here
should be understandable by any other student in MAT133.

research question. Similar to the other sections, this section should be written in a way that is easy for the
reader to follow. For example, please do not include a wall of formulas without providing context. You may

organize this section as you see fit, however, please be sure to include the following elements at some point,
in any order.
• Include at least one calculation of an average rate of change and interpret its meaning in context. (Be
sure to include units in your interpretation!)
• Include at least one estimate of an instantaneous rate of change and interpret its meaning in context.
(Be sure to include units in your interpretation!)
• Include at least one estimate of a relative rate of change and interpret its meaning in context. (Be sure

• Create a data table consisting of (approximate) values of the instantaneous rates of change of the function
represented by your data. Include a professional-looking table of the first 10 rows of this computed data
in your report. Briefly describe how you calculated the numbers in the table.
• Create an approximate graph of the derivative of the function represented by your data. Construct this
graph based only on your dataset and the approximations that you made using this dataset. For this
graph, please do not use a formula that represents the function, even if one exists. Be sure that your
axes are clearly labelled, including units.
• Provide a discussion on the rates of change of your function. Explain how they are relevant to the
“real world” and how they relate to your research question. Relate your findings to the source(s) you
highlighted in your introduction. Your language here should be understandable by any other student in
MAT133.
Once again, please remember that your report should be engaging, comprehensible and coherent. Kindly
refrain from hitting your reader in the face with a wall of formulae! Use full sentences to provide context.
4. Conclusion
In language that is easily accessible to the general public, write paragraph(s) to summarize your findings and
explain how they relate to your research question.
Please ensure that your conclusion also addresses the following questions: What further questions related
to this project would your pod like to investigate if you had more data? Looking at the syllabus, which
upcoming topics in MAT133 might be related to these questions? Which real-world phenomenon might be
related to these questions? Please identify at least one upcoming topic in MAT133 and at least one real-life
phenomenon. Discuss the potential connections between these topics and your further questions.
IMPORTANT: Please make sure to pay special attention to your pod’s writing to ensure its quality and
clarity. This includes integration of mathematics and figures into your paragraphs, ensuring all figures
and graphs are well-labelled, and being mindful of the overall appearance of your submission. A guide for
including mathematical formulas in your reports can be found on Quercus.
Please see the General Project Guidelines and Instructions for submission instructions.
