1 Introduction
The projects in MAT133 have been carefully designed over several years to help you develop professional mathemat-
ical, writing, and presentation skills that will serve you in other courses and your career. This document outlines
general guidelines and helpful tips that apply to every project. You will be supplied with detailed, specific
instruction booklets for each project as they arise throughout the year.
Typically, there are four main pieces to each project: the Individual Part, the Pod Project (including a Pod
Contract, First Draft, and Final Report), the tutorial Presentation, and the Reflection Piece.
The Individual Part helps you build foundational skills that are required for each project and includes an Academic
Integrity Statement and individual problems, which you complete on your own before starting group work. The
Pod Project is the main written piece where you and your pod detail your findings; it begins with a Pod Contract,
followed by a First Draft, and then a Final Report. The Presentation is where your pod shares your findings with
the rest of your Hive during tutorial, through a short slide presentation followed by a hive discussion. Finally, the
Reflection Piece asks you to individually reflect and write on your group work experience during the project.
Each part of the project is to be submitted to Gradescope (embedded within Quercus). Note that you may re-
submit as many times as you like before the submission deadline . If you resubmit the pod project and/or slides,
you will need to re-add your pod members to your submission each time you resubmit.
While projects are graded holistically by the teaching team, each project this year also has a soft-skill focus. Details
on the focus, along with a partial rubric showing how it will be assessed, will be included in the relevant project’s
booklet as the year progresses.

2 Academic Integrity
2.1 The Code and the OK List
The UofT has high standards of academic integrity. It is your responsibility to read and understand the Code of
Behaviour on Academic Matters and to adhere to the list of “OK” resources below for this project.
The OK List
This OK list is a closed list of allowed resources, not just a list of examples. If you are unsure of what you are
allowed to use, do not hesitate to ask on Piazza.
OK: Collaboration with other members of your pod and hive. You are also welcome and encouraged to discuss
the individual questions with your pod, then write up the final draft of your solutions on your own (using
your own reasoning, without copying from another source). You are required to collaborate on the pod
project with your pod.
OK: Anything found on the MAT133 Quercus page and general advice on the MAT133 Piazza page
OK: Your own and your hive member’s previous MAT133 work, including any projects, tests, or homework
OK: Your own and your hive members’ notes from this course and your other courses
OK: Any other textbook (online or physical) you have access to
OK: Online learning videos (e.g. Khan Academy)
OK: Data sources including, but not limited to, those listed in Project Appendices
OK: Using generative AI to review grammar or formatting.
OK: Excel, wolframalpha.com, desmos.com, any calculator
OK: Anything else declared as OK in a written announcement by the course coordinator
Examples of Not OK Things
Here are some examples of things that are not OK. These are just examples. Unless something is on the OK
list, it is not OK.
NO: Using generative AI, such as ChatGPT or Microsoft Copilot, to assist with the Individual Submission
or Reflection Piece, brainstorm research questions, analyze data sets or generate any portion of the Pod
Report.
NO: Communicating about the project with anyone not in your hive. For example, you may not use group
chats to share project content if these group chats involve anyone who is not in your hive.
NO: Asking for answers on Piazza (e.g. “how do you solve Question 2...”)
NO: Accessing or posting on so-called “tutoring” websites or services like chegg.com or Easy 4.0.
NO: Using online forums like stackexchange.
NO: Entering the question text into a search engine.
An important note on group chats: If you have administration privileges for any online chat that involves
anyone not on your team and in which assignment content is shared, you must delete any non-authorized
content from that group chat as soon as you see it (if this is technologically possible on that platform).
Otherwise, you are considered to have helped someone cheat and therefore committed an academic offence.

4 Pod Project
4.1 A Note on Collaboration
Generally, Pod Projects will include 3-4 members that will submit one First Draft and one Final Report together.
You should collaborate as a group on all parts of the pod projects . Before starting a project, have a 5-10
minute conversation about your group work norms, if you haven’t already done so in the tutorials. For example, talk
about upcoming deadlines in other classes, working styles (slow and steady vs. working in large chunks), reasonable
times to expect replies to text messages or emails, etc.
All contributing pod members share the same First Draft, Final Report, and Presentation Slides grades, so it is in
everyone’s best interest to collaborate on all parts of the project — not just the part you are primarily responsible
for writing. While each member may take the lead on one section, the quality of the final submission is a shared
responsibility. Every pod member is expected to contribute to a collaborative working environment by giving
feedback, proofreading each other’s work, and approving the final submission together.
Here are some tips for collaboration, from past students:
• Start early!
• Set soft deadlines in advance of the real deadline, to make sure your pod can finish in time, support each
other if you run into an unexpected snag, and give each other feedback to improve before submission.
• While you will be able to use parts of the tutorial sessions to discuss your project, it is important to set up
additional times to meet in person with your pod to collaborate. Many students find it more efficient to meet
in person than to sort things out only through online messaging, especially in terms of final polishing of the
report and practicing the presentation.
• In addition to your in-person meetings, decide on a convenient method of communication, such as email or
an instant messaging app of your choice, to communicate outside of your meetings.
• Missed your tutorial? If you were unable to attend the tutorial in which pods were assigned, it is your
responsibility to find out who is in your pod and reach out to them as soon as possible. You can find your
pod assignment on Quercus by clicking “Groups” in the left side bar. Do not wait for your pod to contact
you! Reach out proactively, introduce yourself, and let them know you are ready to contribute.
We believe that all students will be supportive and communicative pod collaborators! However, if a student in
your pod does not communicate with their pod before the pod contract deadline and/or does not contribute to
the project before the first draft deadline, then your pod will unfortunately need to move forward with the project
without that student. Only students who contribute to the project should be included on the title page and on the
Gradescope submission. If you have questions about a challenging situation in your pod, please reach out to us at
admin133@math.toronto.edu.
Pod conflicts
If a pod member is unresponsive, please reach out to us no later than the day of the Pod Contract deadline. If
you write to us later than this, there won’t be enough time to resolve the situation, so we will be unable to help.
If your pod experiences a conflict that affects your ability to work together once the project has begun, please
reach out to us as soon as the issue arises. The earlier you contact us, the more we are able to help.
Important: Only one project per pod may be submitted for grading. You are not permitted to subdivide
your pod once it has been assigned.

4.2 Citations and Formatting
When you cite your sources and format your reports, we ask you to please use the American Psychological Associ-
ation (APA) style. For a brief guide on using the APA citation style, please see the guide to formulating research
questions, using proper citations and formatting reports. There is also an abundance of useful information on APA
formatting available online on the American Psychological Association website.
All mathematical expressions must be typeset using an equation editor rather than written as plain text. For a
guide on integrating mathematical symbols and formulas into your report in a professional manner, please see our
guide for including mathematical formulas in your reports and presentations on Quercus.

Your First Draft should be a mostly complete version of your pod project. The more complete your draft, the more

Category Description

5 Presentation
5.1 Presentation Instructions
The presentation is based on your pod project. Your pod will present your project to the rest of your hive. Each
member of your pod will briefly discuss a part of the project.
Your pod presentation will generally consist of two pieces: your slide presentation and the hive discussion. In
order to present, your TA will be sharing a PDF of your presentation to the screen via HDMI.
Slide presentation: Your slide presentation will be a maximum of 8 minutes long. That means each member of
your pod should speak for about 2 minutes. The 8 minute limit will be enforced, so that all pods have sufficient
time to present. Make sure to practice beforehand so you don’t overrun.
Hive discussion: After your presentation, your hive will have about 5 minutes to discuss your presentation and
ask questions (depending on how many pods are in your hive). To get the conversation started, your pod must
prepare at least 1-2 guiding questions. You are responsible for managing the hive discussion after your presentation.
Your pod will need to prepare a slideshow (PowerPoint, Google Slides, Canva, etc.) to display while you are pre-
sentating. This should include any important information and visual representations you created in your project.
You will submit your slides to Gradescope along with your Final Report. Your slides must be submitted in
the form of a PDF document, so avoid using animations. If you only provide a link to access your slides
(e.g. in Google Drive or OneDrive), they will not be graded.

Here are some tips on preparing and delivering presentations:
• Practice beforehand! Set up a meeting with your pod and practice the entire presentation. Make sure
that everyone is comfortable with their part and that the presentation doesn’t take more than 8 minutes.
Make sure the person sharing slides knows when to go to the next slide!
• Use cue cards, not phones. You may use cue cards to guide your presentation, but avoid writing your
entire script as it leads to reading and stifles engagement with your audience. For professionalism, avoid using
electronic devices (phones, tablets, etc.) while you present, and please make eye contact with your hive!
• Don’t speak too quickly. If you speak too quickly, your hive will find it difficult to understand you. It is
better to focus on the key points from your project than try to discuss every detail.
• Do not pack your slides with lots of information! The most effective slides are concise. Each slide may
include images, figures, and/or a few brief bullet points, but it should never contain full paragraphs.
• Include visual aids. Make your presentation interesting by including supporting exhibits. When you include
graphs, tables, and/or images into your presentation, make sure that they are well-labelled, including titles,
captions, axis labels, and units.
5.2 Presentation Grading Rubric
Your presentation grade (generally out of 10 in total, with an exception for Project 3 (more details to come)) will
be based on your individual performance as well as your pod performance and slides , as outlined below.
Individual Performance (out of 5):
• Presence (1): Arrive on time and don’t leave early. Give your full attention to the presentation at hand; put
your phone and any other devices away, so as not to be distracted.
• Discussion (1): Engage in hive discussion after each pod’s presentation. Ask questions! Give feedback.
• Mathematics (1): Discuss any relevant concepts from the course, using accurate mathematical language.
• Coherence (1): Your pod’s presentation must be coherent. Your segment of the presentation should connect
with the rest of your pod’s presentation.
• Accessibility to Audience (1): The rest of your hive should be able to understand your presentation. Avoid
using unnecessary jargon.
Pod Performance and Slides (5):
• Mathematics (2): You must relate your pod’s project to relevant concepts from the course and use precise
mathematical language.
• Content (1): Your slideshow should summarise the work your pod did in your project and how this answers
the questions in the project instructions.
• Style (2): Your slides should not be overly packed with information. They should show clear graphs or other
visuals where possible. Text should consist of concise bullet points, rather than paragraphs.
Your individual performance marks will be posted to Quercus, whereas your pod performance will be provided on
your slides in Gradescope.
Are you ready for your pod presentation? Use the checklist below to be sure.
□ Slides are submitted as a PDF to Gradescope by the Tuesday deadline.
□ You have practiced, on your own and with your pod, and your presentation does not exceed 8 minutes.
□ You are able to deliver your presentation without reading a script.
□ A pod member has been designated to switch between slides.
□ Discussion questions are written and ready for you to start the discussion with your hive.

6 Reflection Piece
Reflection is key to learning from our experiences. As usual, you will complete a weekly reflection in Gradescope.
The questions posted on the Friday after your presentations will be based on your experiences with the project.
Your grade on the reflection piece will be based on your efforts to thoughtfully reflect on each question, according to
the instructions provided. While we do ask that you write in full English sentences, we will not judge your grammar
or syntax. Please don’t worry too much about writing perfectly! Think of it as being like a journal entry.
The estimated time to appropriately complete these questions is approximately 30 minutes.
