1 Welcome to the MAT133 Community!
We believe that a vibrant and supportive community of students, TAs, and instructors is funda-
mental to your learning experience in MAT133. Welcome to the community!
To help you connect and collaborate with each other, the
course community is structured on a system of hives and
pods. Your hive is a group of up to 16 students and a dedi-
cated TA that you’ll work closely with throughout the year.
Hives work together in both lectures and tutorials, how-
ever, your tutorial is your designated weekly meeting time
with your hive and hive TA only. Within your hive, you’ll
also have a pod of 3-4 students, with whom you’ll col-
laborate more closely on projects, presentations, and other
classwork. Hives remain consistent throughout the year,
whereas your pods may change periodically.

2 Course Content and Learning Goals
MAT133Y1 is designed for students with an interest in commerce or the social sciences, in order
to provide the mathematical foundation needed to embark on their future studies and careers. We
will focus on topics in linear algebra, differential calculus and integral calculus, in one and two
variables, and their applications in business and economics.
Learning objectives are provided for each course module; these clarify what you should be able to
do after engaging in all learning activities for this module, including lectures, homework, projects,
and other forms of study. We will also be constantly aiming to develop the following big-picture
skills:
• flexibility in mathematical thinking : the ability to translate between different representa-
tions of mathematical ideas, including graphs, verbal descriptions, data, and formulas;
• problem-solving skills needed to solve new problems that you haven’t seen before, espe-
cially the types of real-world problems that you’ll encounter in your future studies and ca-
reers;
• mathematical communication: using written, verbal, and visual explanations to commu-
nicate mathematical ideas, for the dual purpose of presenting to others and developing a
deeper understanding for yourself.

6.3 E-mail Etiquette
If you need to email us, please consider the following.
• Be professional and respectful. When emailing instructors, always write from your mail.
utoronto.ca email address and summarize the topic about which you are writing in the
subject line. Begin your email with “Dear Professor. . . ” and end by signing off with your
name and student ID number.
• Please refrain from asking math-based questions via email. We are much better able to
support you on your math-based questions through Drop-in Study Hours or through Piazza,
rather than email! However, if you are having any trouble with accessing Drop-in Study
Hours or Piazza, then please email us at admin133@math.toronto.edu.
• Check the syllabus first. If you have a question about the course policies, check the syl-
labus first. If you still can’t find your answer, please reach out to us in Piazza or at ad-
min133@math.toronto.edu.
• Use Piazza instead if you can. If you have a question or request that does not involve any
confidential or personal information, then we recommend posting it on Piazza. Instructors
appreciate this because it will likely reduce the number of emails we need to respond to
about the same question and your fellow students will also appreciate seeing the answer
without having to ask!
• If you must email us, use admin133@math.toronto.edu , rather than your instructor’s per-
sonal email address. We have set up a ticketing system on this email account that helps us to
keep track of the many emails we receive from a class of over 1250 students, to ensure that
we respond in a timely fashion. Instructors and administrative work together to respond to
your emails.
• Please don’t send us duplicates of the same question. Do not post to Piazza and email us
with the same question at the same time. This will slow down our response time.
• Don’t open multiple tickets about the same issue.If you email admin133@math.toronto.edu
about an issue and you need to send us a second email about the same issue, then pleasere-
ply to the automated response to your initial message. This will save our time and ensure
that we will read both of your messages before responding.
• Be patient. We will respond to emails as quickly as possible, but during busy times, it might
take several days to respond, especially if your request requires special efforts from our
team. We won’t lose your emails to admin133@math.toronto.edu, but if you want to send a
follow-up message on the same issue, please push reply to use the same subject line, rather
than starting a new thread, as described above.

7 Course Activities
7.1 Overview
The main activities you’ll engage with in MAT133 are briefly summarized below. Further details
and updates on these activities will be provided through the course Quercus page.
• Lectures: Lectures will be taught in anactive-learning style, which means that you can expect
to be an active participant in class, exploring new ideas in collaboration with your peers. All
lectures are held on Mondays, Wednesdays, and Fridays.
• Checkpoints: After each lecture, we’ll recommend a few short problems in that week’s
Checkpoint workbook for you to complete to check your understanding on the concepts
from last class and prepare you for next class. We recommend you complete these by the
evening before your next lecture. However, these are not for credit and are not to be submit-
ted, so there is no formal deadline. Each week’s Checkpoint workbook will be available on
Quercus.
• Weekly Homework: Each week, students who choose to participate in the online homework
component will complete a homework assignment online in WileyPLUS. The WileyPLUS
homework is intended to support learning through regular practice. You will be allowed
several attempts at each question and you won’t be penalized for mistakes, as long as you
correct them within the allowed attempts.
• Weekly Reflections: Each week you’ll also complete a brief written reflection on your expe-
riences in the course. These are intended both to aid in your learning and also to provide
feedback to the teaching team on your experiences. For both purposes, your reflection needs
to be as authentic as possible and written in your own words. For this reason, we will not
assess your writing style or grammar when grading your reflections.
• Projects: Projects are key to learning through application and experience in MAT133. Each
project will contain a number of parts, some of which will be completed individually while
other parts will be completed in collaboration with your pod. More information about the
projects will be posted on Quercus.

• Hive Meetings and Presentations (T utorials): You will meet with your hive of up to 16

your projects. After project deadlines, your pod will present your results to your hive and
TA. All hive meetings and presentations are mandatory to attend.

Faculty of Arts and Science in the Winter semester.

to connect with fellow students and an instructor or TA to talk about anything MAT133-
related. Piazza is an online messaging board, where you can post questions and discuss
other students’ questions.
• Math Learning Centre: The Math Learning Centre (MLC) is another space for finding peers
and TAs to work with, provided by the UofT Department of Mathematics. More information
about the MLC will be provided in Quercus.

Further details about how you are assessed in each component are described below.
• Weekly Homework: Students who choose to participate in the online homework component
will complete Weekly Homework assignments in WileyPLUS. As noted in Section 7, you
will be allowed plenty of attempts on each problem and you will receive full points on each
problem, as long as you solve it correctly within the allowed number of attempts. Any
problem submitted after the deadline will receive a 25% penalty. The 25% penalty is applied
only to individual problems submitted late, not to the full homework assignment. In the case
that you were unable to complete a homework assignment by its deadline, it will always
help your score to complete the remaining problems after the deadline.
Students who choose to opt out of the Weekly Homework component will not receive a
Weekly Homework grade. Instead, the 6% normally allocated to Weekly Homework will
be added to the term tests, increasing the weight of each term test by 2%. Although par-
ticipation in Weekly Homework is optional, it is designed to provide regular practice and
feedback that many students find helpful in preparing for tests and exams. Students who
wish to opt out of the Weekly Homework may do so by completing this form by Friday,
October 23rd.
• Weekly Reflections: Your weekly reflections will be graded based on effort to answer the
questions provided, as well as adherence to provided word count minima. We will not
assess your writing style or grammar. We are not able to accept late submissions, however,
we will automatically drop your lowest two reflection scores to accommodate for illness or

8.4 “Curving”
The UofT does not grade “on the curve”. In other words, there are no quotas for the number of
As, Bs, etc. Your mark will represent your mastery of the learning objectives of the course, not
your performance relative to your peers. For example, if all students truly master all learning
objectives, then all students in the course will earn an A! This is the situation we all strive for.
We want to emphasize that you are not competing for the top marks with your peers . It is in
everyone’s best interest if you work together to succeed, rather than compete with each other.
There may be rare instances where adjustments to grades are required, to ensure that we are ad-
hering to UofT’s grading policy. The instructors and the Dean reserve the right to make such
adjustments, if necessary. However, such adjustments will never be based on quotas. These ad-
justments are also extremely rare; no such adjustments have been applied in MAT133 for at least
5 years.

15.1 Four First-Year Calculus Streams
The Faculty of Arts and Science offers four different first-year calculus streams. Even though
MAT133Y1 is designed primarily for students in Rotman Commerce or the Social Sciences, some
of these students choose to enrol in one of the other calculus streams instead. Here is a brief
comparison of MAT133Y1 with the each of the other calculus streams.
• MAT130H1/135H1+MAT136H1:MAT133Y1 and MAT130/5H1 +MAT136H1 bear many sim-
ilarities. In both courses, students learn the fundamentals of single-variable differential
and integral calculus, with a focus on applications to real-world problems. The applica-
tions in MAT133 tend to be more business/economics-oriented, while the applications in
MAT130+MAT136 are more life-science-oriented and the applications in MAT135+MAT136
21
--- Page 22 ---
are oriented toward the other sciences. There are some additional mathematical topics that
are unique to each course: MAT130/5+MAT136, you would learn about the convergence
of sequences and series, as well as modelling with differential equations. In MAT133Y1,
you’ll learn about systems of linear equations and matrices, as well as differentiation and
optimization for functions of two variables.
• MAT148H1+MAT149H1 also covers single-variable differential and integral calculus, but
with a focus on developing mathematical logic and proof skills. There is less emphasis on
building intuition and problem-solving skills needed for applications than in MAT133Y1.
Unlike MAT133, MAT148+MAT149 does not include any multivariable calculus or linear
algebra.
• MAT158H1+MAT159H1is similar to MAT148+MAT149 in that it is more focused on theory
than application, but it is somewhat faster and more focused on theory. It starts by construct-
ing the real numbers, for example. Unlike MAT133, MAT158+MAT159 does not include any
multivariable calculus or linear algebra.
If you are a student in Rotman Commerce specifically, any of the above calculus streams will serve
as a valid prerequisite for future core courses in the specialization of your choice. Is MAT133Y1
right for you? Here are a few of the pros and cons of MAT133Y1, relative to the other calculus
streams, for a typical Rotman Commerce student. Please note that this list may not be complete!
Be sure to reach out to your academic advisor or professor if you are having trouble deciding
which stream to take.
• Pros of MAT133Y1: For Rotman Commerce students, the two main advantages of taking
MAT133Y1 are: (1) we explicitly focus on business/economics applications and (2) we dis-
cuss a selection of mathematical topics that will best prepare you for your future studies in
business and economics.
• Cons of MAT133Y1:There are some upper-level courses at UofT that require one of the other
calculus courses as a prerequisite. These courses are not required by the Rotman Commerce
program, but you might still be interested in taking them. This includes some proof-based
upper-level courses in mathematics and statistics. If you are interested in focusing more
on mathematical proofs rather than applications, you might consider MAT148+MAT149 in-
stead.
If you are unsure, we recommend talking with a professor or academic advisor about which cal-
culus course might best suit your needs.
